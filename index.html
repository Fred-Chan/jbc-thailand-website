About the challenge

Robotics that behaves like the real world

The Junior Botball Challenge is an international educational robotics competition built on the Botball program — a methodology with roots in MIT engineering education and a long history of support from the wider robotics and space-science community. Teams are given a mission mat, a set of objects and a strict time limit, and must make a robot solve the course entirely on its own. No remote control, no second chances mid-run: whatever the students programmed is what the robot does.

The Thailand Open is the Southeast Asian stage of that series. It is deliberately designed so that a nine-year-old attempting their first line-following routine and a teenager optimising sensor fusion can compete on the same floor, in different divisions, on the same day. The scoring rewards reliability over spectacle — a modest robot that finishes every run cleanly will beat an ambitious one that fails under pressure, which is precisely the engineering lesson the event exists to teach.

What students actually take away

  • Programming under constraint — writing logic that must survive a noisy, physical environment.
  • Mechanical iteration — rebuilding a gripper three times in one morning because the floor friction changed.
  • Debugging in public — diagnosing failure calmly, with a referee and a crowd watching.
  • Cross-border teamwork — sharing a pit floor with teams from across Asia and beyond.

Divisions

Three levels, one arena

Teams enter the division that matches their experience, and each division is scored and awarded separately — so a first-year team is never measured against a veteran one.

01

Rookie

For newcomers. Teams focus on getting a reliable robot moving accurately along the mission mat and completing the foundational tasks within the time limit.

Entry point · no prior experience needed
02

Advanced

For teams with a season or more behind them. Harder object handling, tighter tolerances and strategic choices about which missions to attempt and which to skip.

Returning teams · strategy matters
03

Expert

The sharp end. Sensor-driven precision, custom mechanisms and run-to-run consistency decide the podium; marginal gains in calibration separate gold from silver.

Experienced competitors · top tier

What the 2026 Bangkok edition looked like

On 3 May 2026, the Junior Botball Challenge Asia — Thailand Open was staged at King's College International School Bangkok. The day opened with a traditional Thai khon masked-dance performance, filled the school's indoor arena with competition mats from wall to wall, and closed with an award ceremony for every division. More than 700 participants and guests attended, and a large contingent of students went on to the Junior Botball Challenge Global Finals in Beijing later that year.

For teams travelling in

Built to be entered from abroad

The Thailand Open is run in English, in a city with one of Asia's best-connected airports, at a campus that is used to hosting international visitors. Everything below is what an overseas coach normally needs to settle before committing a team — tell us where you are flying from and we will send the specifics for your country in the invitation pack.

  1. 1
    Register your interest

    Send us your school or club name, country, number of teams and the age range of your students. No payment at this stage.

  2. 2
    Receive the invitation pack

    Confirmed dates, the official rules and mission description for the season, division guidance, fees and the entry deadline.

  3. 3
    Plan travel and documents

    We issue the supporting invitation letter teams commonly need for visa applications and for school approval, plus guidance on recommended hotels near the venue.

  4. 4
    Prepare and compete

    Practice against the published mission, arrive for check-in and calibration, then compete and attend the award ceremony the same day.

Do we need Botball experience?

No. The Rookie division exists precisely for teams entering their first season. Coaches receive the mission description in advance so students can practise before travelling.

What language is the event run in?

English. Briefings, referee communication, scoring and the ceremonies are all conducted in English.

How many students per team?

Teams are small — a handful of students working together on one robot — and each team must travel with a responsible adult coach. Exact team-size limits are confirmed in the invitation pack for each season.

Can we get a letter for visas or school approval?

Yes. Once your entry is confirmed, the organising team issues an official invitation letter naming your school, your students and the event dates.

Can parents and teachers attend?

Yes. The arena has spectator space and the award ceremony is open to accompanying family and staff.

The wider series

Bangkok is a stage, not the finish line

The Thailand Open belongs to the Junior Botball Challenge Asia series, which feeds into the Junior Botball Challenge Global Finals held each year in Beijing, China. The Global Finals draw thousands of competitors from more than a dozen countries and regions, and have been staged at venues including the National Speed Skating Oval — the "Ice Ribbon" of the 2022 Winter Olympics. For a team, that means one season can begin on a school floor in Bangkok and end in an Olympic arena.
